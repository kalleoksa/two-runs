AI in design & development · two runs · v1

One brief. Two processes. Your two decisions.

A team has to build self-service cancellation. Here the same job is delivered twice: once the way most teams work today, and once with AI in the loop. You walk the second run stage by stage and make the two decisions a real team would argue about — what to do with the prototypes, and how much to write down before anyone starts building. What those decisions cost turns up in the strips and the numbers as you go.

Nothing here is measured. Every figure comes from the assumptions at the bottom of the page, which you can change, and the AI-assisted run is free to come out the more expensive one.

Brief
Self-service subscription cancellation for a consumer service (web + app), replacing a phone-and-email-only process.
Why
Cancellations are roughly a third of contact-centre volume. Regulators in two markets now require a self-service path. Trust cost of the current process is high.
Team
PM, product designer, service designer (part-time), content designer (part-time), frontend dev, backend dev, QA, tech lead (review only).
Not why
AI is not the reason for the project. It changes how the work is done, not whether it is worth doing.

The two runs · width is time spent

Each strip below is one whole run of the project, laid out left to right in the order the work happens, with one block per stage. Width is time, so the wider strip is the more expensive run.

Probe — one of the rough, throwaway versions built at stage 3 for the team to argue with, which is where that stage's name comes from.
Rework loop — a problem created at Specify or earlier that nobody finds until Build or later reopens the stage it came from. The arrow points back at that stage, and the run is charged the assumed rework loop cost on top of what the problem itself cost.
Escaped — no stage caught it before release, so a customer met it first. Charged at the assumed production multiplier, and drawn as the outlined block at the end of each strip rather than inside a stage, because finding something after release is not a stage of either process.
