AI in design & development · the spine · v1

The spec is the hinge.

Upstream of it, work is judgement and cannot be delegated. Downstream, work is execution against a written target. The spec is what decides which side a task falls on — so it is the one stage worth over-investing in, and the one this process is built around.

4

Decide

Continue, pivot, or drop. Written down, with the reason. This is the gate AI cannot stand in.

Human decides
5

Specify — three readers, one intent

One decision, written three ways for three audiences. Same content, different reader. If the three disagree, the work upstream is not finished.

5.1

Human-readable

Reader: the team

  • User flows
  • States and error cases
  • UX copy
  • Accessibility requirements
  • Business cases
5.2

Machine-readable

Reader: the model

  • Intent
  • Inputs
  • Expected outputs
  • Known limitations
  • Conventions and patterns to follow
5.3

Testable

Reader: the check

  • Acceptance criteria
  • Test scenarios
  • Failure states
  • Performance expectations

Stop rule: if 5.2 and 5.3 cannot be written, stage 6 does not start. Not because the process says so — because unverifiable output is what makes AI expensive rather than fast.

Downstream · execution against a written target
6

Build

Agents against the machine-readable spec, inside repo conventions. Scaffolding, glue, mechanical change, variants at volume.

Delegate, then review
7

Test

Rule-checkable first: types, lint, unit, accessibility rules. Bug hunting and coverage expansion. Taste and security stay human.

Delegate, then review
8

Release & measure

A/B tests, monitoring, analytics, feedback clustering. Cycle time and rework rate, not perceived speed.

AI drafts, human owns
9

Record

What was delegated, what came back wrong, what the review cost. The only stage that makes the next loop cheaper.

Human decides

The return path. Stage 9 feeds 0 and 2 — not as a decorative arrow, as the actual input. Two things travel back: evidence (what shipped, what it did) and failures (which delegations were not worth the review). Without the second, the process only ever accumulates optimism.

What this deliberately fixes

Stage 9 exists
Your board had the right question — "have we done it, and what did we learn?" — as a template field that is empty nearly everywhere. A field nobody fills is not a process step. Making it a stage with an owner is the change.
The spec triad is promoted
It was a sub-branch of one step. It is the mechanism the whole thing runs on, so it gets the weight.
Synthetic users are a probe, not evidence
Simulated personas and AI-generated journeys belong in stage 3 for cheap coverage of cases you would not have thought of. They cannot close stage 4. Keep the line visible or it will be crossed quietly.
Stakeholder columns are gone
Teams are not stages. Each stage names who decides; which team that is varies by project and does not belong in the map.
